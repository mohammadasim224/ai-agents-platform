# Sales Training Masterclass: Appointment Setting & Closing Manual

---

## SECTION 1: CORE PSYCHOLOGICAL & NON-VERBAL FOUNDATIONS (ALL ROLES)

### 1.1 The 6 Human Needs Psychology
Every human being operates under six fundamental human needs: **Certainty**, **Uncertainty/Variety**, **Significance**, **Love & Connection**, **Growth**, and **Contribution**. While everyone possesses all six needs, individuals value and prioritize them differently.
* **The Fundamental Need Principle**: People will do anything to meet their need, whereas they will only do some things to meet a want. 
* **Primary Drivers**: Significance and Certainty are the two most common needs that prospects value above all else.
* **Execution**: Assess what need the prospect is trying to meet by listening intently to their languaging. Shift their focus and languaging throughout the conversation to meet their specific need rather than attempting to sell everyone using a single rigid angle.

### 1.2 Non-Verbal Execution, Tonality & Body Language
Facial expressions act as the "remote control to your tone." Pacing, body language, and tonality drive execution and command status on every call:
* **Casual / Confident Tone**: Used during Intent and Pitching. Keep your vital organs exposed (chest and stomach area open) and keep your hands visible at all times on camera to signal safety, high status, and lack of threat.
* **Curious Tone**: Used during Discovery and Logical Certainty. Tilt your head and squint your eyes slightly while asking questions.
* **Skeptical Tone**: Used during Rationale, Past Experience, and Calling Out Excuses. Scrunch your eyebrows, lean back/out, and put your hand up to challenge vague answers.
* **Concerned / Empathetic Tone**: Used during Consequence and Cost of Inaction. Lean in, raise your eyebrows, and significantly slow down your verbal pacing.

### 1.3 Verbal Queuing & Mirror Questions
When a prospect gives a vague answer, dodges a question, or displays an ego, **never move on without getting the exact answer required by the process**. Moving on without an answer subconsciously teaches the prospect that you are low status and that your questions do not matter.
* **Step 1**: Call out the fact that they did not answer the question.
* **Step 2**: Ask the same core question using a different phrasing (a mirror question) so they do not feel treated like a toddler, while still securing the required dataset.

## SECTION 2: APPOINTMENT SETTING TRACK (OUTBOUND CALLS TO OPT-IN LEADS)

Designed specifically for outbound appointment setters calling leads who filled out a form, attended a webinar, or opted in for information.

### Stage 1: Intent & Initial Greeting
* **The Greeting Rule**: Never say *"Hey is this [Name]?"* because asking if it is them implies that you do not know them and immediately raises their sales guard. Say *"Hey [Name]"* fast, sharp, and confident so it sounds like an acquaintance calling.
* **Verbatim Opening Script**:
  > *"Hey hey is this [Name]? Yeah what's up buddy, it's just [Setter Name] here from [Company Name]. It looks like you opted in about possible help with lowering your power bill and getting solar on the home, is that about right?"*
  > *(Prospect confirms)*
  > *"Easy man, well just to see if I could help, because I might be able to send you like a bit of a more specific breakdown for your home, what was your main intent behind I guess even looking into solar in the first place? What were you hoping to get out of it?"*

### Stage 2: Experience & Logical Certainty (Current Utility Strategy)
* **Uncover Tangible (Goal)**: Identify their overarching target (e.g., locking in fixed energy costs, eliminating bill spikes).
* **Experience (Past Problem)**:
  > *"What have you seen that I guess makes you feel like you're paying way too much on your utility bills right now?"*
* **Logical Certainty (Current Strategy & Root Cause)**:
  > *"Well, what are you doing now in terms of your power usage that you feel like isn't allowing you to keep those bills down?"*
  > *"And how long have you been dealing with those high rates from the utility company?"*
  > *"And what caused you to just stay with the utility company in the first place?"*
* **The "Do You Like" Sequence**:
  > *"Do you like it though? Like do you like relying on the utility company for all your power?"*
  > *(If they say no, force to a yes)*: *"Well it can't all be terrible, like what do you like about just paying the power company every month?"*
  > *(Transition after yes)*: *"Okay, it doesn't sound like terrible then, but is there anything you would change about either the utility company or the monthly rate you get from them if you could though?"*
* **Probing the Problem**:
  > *"What do you mean by that?"*
  > *"How long has that been going on for to where you're just throwing money at the utility company?"*
  > *"And without assuming anything, you paying those rising electric rates for the past [X] years, has that had an impact on your monthly budget or peace of mind in any way?"*

### Stage 3: Qualification & Booking the Closer
* **Willingness to Invest / Transition**:
  > *"In an ideal world, where do you want to be in six months in terms of your power costs?"*
  > *"Let me ask you, if there was a way to lock in a fixed lower rate and own your power instead of renting it, would you actually be willing to make a change if that's what it took?"*
  > *(Prospect confirms)*
  > *"Okay easy man. Well based on what I've heard, obviously you mentioning that your electric bills are spiking and you're tired of renting your power, I feel like there might be a possibility of being able to help you. What I can do from here is connect you with [Closer Name], our senior solar specialist. He can dive a lot deeper into your current roof and energy usage, and if he feels we can help, he can walk you through potential options. How does that sound, would that be awful?"*

## SECTION 3: CLOSING TRACK (ONLINE VIRTUAL & IN-HOME SALES MEETINGS)

Designed for closers conducting online Zoom/Google Meet presentations or face-to-face sales meetings at the homeowner's table.

### Stage 1: Intent & Pre-Handling Rationale
* **Recap**:
  > *"Hey [Name], I know you spoke with [Setter Name] earlier and you kind of mentioned that your electric bills have been climbing and it's super inconsistent—that's kind of where you're at in a nutshell, right?"*
* **Rationale Question (Pre-handling "I'll do it myself / wait")**:
  > *"Besides obviously wanting to eliminate that monthly power bill, what's the main reason you're looking at getting a custom solar setup put in place rather than just continuing to pay the utility company like the average person does?"*
* **Pre-handling Past Action & Objections**:
  > *"Before you and I were speaking, what have you actually done about looking into solar or getting control of your energy costs?"*
  > *(If nothing)*: *"What prevented you man, why not?"*
  > *(Prospect gives past objection e.g., money, timing, spouse)*
  > *"Well out of curiosity, what shifted for you now then, that actually has you open to looking at getting this taken care of?"*

### Stage 2: Emotional Certainty, Future Pacing & Consequence
* **Usain Bolt Analogy (Pull & Push Frame)**:
  > *"Can I offer you a perspective man? Have you heard the Usain Bolt analogy? If you take Usain Bolt, the fastest man in the world, and you put him on a starting line and tell him to run a 100-meter dash, that motherfucker going to run fast. Now you put a lion behind him chasing him down trying to eat him, he's going to run even faster. Because the most successful people in the world, they have a strong pull of what they want to achieve, but they also have a strong enough consequence to failure to push them to actually achieve it."*
* **Future Pacing (The Pull)**:
  > *"Let's say there was a way of possibly helping you with completely eliminating that electric bill and putting you in control of your power—what would tangibly be like different for you at that point?"*
  > *(Probe deep)*: *"Where could you spend that extra money instead? How would that feel, put yourself in those shoes for a second?"*
* **Consequence of Inaction (The Push / The Lion)**:
  > *"And on the flip side, what if you don't man? What happens if we stay on the exact same trajectory where we keep paying rising utility rates for the next 2 days, 2 weeks, 2 months, even 2 years man—what happens at that point?"*
  > *"And what is the day-to-day feeling for you 3, 6, 12 months from now knowing that you could have locked in your power costs, but you didn't?"*
  > *(Target Answer: Regret)*

### Stage 3: 3-Pillar Pitch & Closing
* **Transition Questions**:
  > *"Are you willing to continue settling for that reality?"*
  > *"And why draw the line in the sand now?"*
  > *"And whose responsibility do you feel like it is to make sure your home and family are protected financially?"*
* **The 3-Pillar Pitch Structure**:
  > *"Pillar one is [Net Metering & Production Protection] because you mentioned earlier how your bills spike in the summer... Pillar two is [Ownership & Fixed Monthly Rate] because you mentioned earlier how you hate throwing money away to a landlord utility company... Pillar three is [Full Warranty & System Monitoring] because you mentioned earlier wanting zero surprise repair expenses..."*
* **Buying Commitment & Price Drop**:
  > *"Now based on everything we've covered man, do you actually feel like this structure would get you from where you are to where you want to go?"*
  > *"Okay, then why though? What do you feel is really like the key to the castle for you?"*
  > *"Okay cool man. Well based on everything I've heard, the total investment to put you in a position to eliminate that bill is [Amount]. How would you like to proceed?"*

## SECTION 4: THE OBJECTION HANDLING MATRIX STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE

When handling objections, classify them into three distinct types: **Partner-Based**, **Money/Logistical**, or **Fear-Based**. Diagnose the specific objection type first, then execute the exact corresponding handling sequence.

### Objection Classification & Sequential Workflow

1. **Partner-Based Objections**:
   * Establish same-side positioning with the prospect.
   * Determine whether the partner is on board.
   * If partner is on board: Go directly for a soft close and proceed.
   * If partner is not on board: Shift ultimate decision-making responsibility back onto the prospect.
   * If prospect remains unwilling to proceed without partner: Schedule a structured follow-up meeting with both decision-makers present.

2. **Money-Based Objections**:
   * Confirm that the objection is purely logistical and not a hidden value objection.
   * Guide the prospect to identify available financial solutions (savings, cash flow).
   * If savings are unavailable: Explore leveraging credit, financing, or reallocated utility savings.
   * Once a financial arrangement is determined: Seal the logistical objection closed and transition to fear handling if hesitation remains.

3. **Fear-Based Objections**:
   * Execute sequential psychological reframes (Decision-Making Process, Certainty/Perspective, 4000 Dots, The Island).
   * Ramp up emotional intensity across loops until securing buying commitment or conducting a clean exit.

---

### 4.1 Partner-Based Objections (Spousal Support)
* **Step 1: Get on the same side of the table**:
  > **Salesperson**: *"Okay, so if your partner was here right now and all for it, would this possibly be the answer for you?"*
  > **Prospect**: *"Yeah, I think it would."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"Okay, but why? What specifically do you feel is the key to the castle for you?"*
  > *(Prospect articulates goal/motivation)*
  > **Salesperson**: *"Okay. So how does [partner] feel about you having the right system to make sure your home eliminates [$ goal] in electric bills over the next 12 months?"*
  > *(Prospect describes partner's stance)*
  > **Salesperson**: *"Okay. Well, what are you going to do if they don't want you to move forward so you can get to that level financially?"*
* **Step 2: Shift responsibility back onto the prospect**:
  > **Salesperson**: *"If I can offer a suggestion... I'm a big believer in 'with great power comes great responsibility'... because whoever has a goal also holds the burden if it doesn't get achieved. For example, if somebody wants to lose 50lbs, it's their responsibility to lose it because it's their weight. So out of curiosity, how much involvement does [partner] have with managing the utility bills? Will you guys be doing this together?"*
  > **Prospect**: *"No, not really."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"Can I ask you a question? Is it sort of fair to make someone who doesn't bear the weight of the responsibility... who can't control the outcome... to be the one who TRULY makes the decision? Because if [partner] says no, you don't do it."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"So what do you feel we should do to put you in the best position to save [$ goal] over the next 12 months?"*
* **Heavy is the Head Reframe (Alternative Partner Reframe)**:
  > **Salesperson**: *"Have you heard the saying like heavy is the head that wears the crown? The idea of it is like the weight of the crown isn't the crown itself... the weight of the crown is the decisions you make when you have that crown on. Because back in the day the king, he was responsible for hundreds of thousands of lives because as the king it would not be fair to put the burden of that responsibility on anybody but himself. For you, who's going to be the one actually managing the utility bills and handling the payments? Is that going to be you or your partner?"*
  > **Prospect**: *"It's going to be me."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"So whose responsibility is it to really put your home in the best possible position?"*

### 4.2 Money / Logistical Objections
* **Step 1: Remove value objections**:
  > **Salesperson**: *"Tell me, money aside, do you feel this would work for you?"*
  > **Prospect**: *"Yeah, I think it would."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"Why do you feel it would though?"*
  > **Salesperson**: *"Is it just the upfront investment or if we could split it up into more digestible payments, would that help?"*
* **Step 2: Uncover financial solutions**:
  > **Salesperson**: *"So cash on hand, bills and expenses aside, what do you have available so you can get started—just to see if I can help?"*
  > *(If savings is not an option)*: *"Have you ever considered leveraging credit or funding to join and just paying it off with the extra savings you make on your electric bill?"*
  > *(If credit is not an option)*: *"What other ways do you think you might have to get the investment required?"*
* **Step 3: Seal objection closed**:
  > **Salesperson**: *"Ok so with the understanding that [payment plan] plan works within budgetary constraints and you feel it will get you to your goal of [$ goal]... how would you like to proceed?"*

### 4.3 Fear Objections & Reframes
Once logistical objections are settled, loop through fear reframes:

* **Reframe 1: Decision-Making Process**:
  > **Salesperson**: *"Yeah that makes sense, would you agree our mindset affects our decision-making process?"*
  > **Prospect**: *"Yes, of course."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"Ok and on a scale of 1 to 10 - 1 being mediocre, 10 being insanely good, where do we feel our energy situation is right now?"*
  > **Prospect**: *"Probably around a 3."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"Why not a 10?"*
  > **Prospect**: *"Because my electric bills keep rising and I have no control over the utility company."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"Ok and do you want the decision making process that got us to being a 3, to dictate the home finances moving forward?"*
  > **Prospect**: *"No, I don't."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"So what decision do you feel you have to make to put yourself in the best position?"*

* **Reframe 2: Certainty / Perspective (McDonald's vs Entrepreneur)**:
  > **Salesperson**: *"Well that makes sense man, because like ultimately what you're seeking is like certainty, right? It's kind of like this: if I have a McDonald's worker who makes $2,000 a month every single month, do you think that's somebody who's after uncertainty and adventure, or safety and certainty?"*
  > **Prospect**: *"Obviously safety and certainty."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"And on the other end, if I have an entrepreneur who takes massive risk and gets the payoff, do you think that's somebody after certainty, or a little adventure and taking a shot on themselves?"*
  > **Prospect**: *"Taking a shot on themselves."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"Can you see how you making your decisions based off seeking certainty could have been what put you in this position where you've been paying rising bills for years? So what decision do you feel like you need to make out of uncertainty to put yourself in the best position?"*

* **Reframe 3: The 4,000 Dots Reframe**:
  > **Salesperson**: *"Ok i gotcha, I'll explain it this way. I have a calendar at my office with 4000 dots. Do you know what those dots represent? A week... so according to scientists, an average human lives for about 4000 weeks and so every time a week passes I go up and circle a dot. At 40yrs old half the dots are gone. Another 1/3 will go to sleep. That leaves us with give or take 1250 weeks left... How much time has gone by not being where you want to be by not taking action?"*
  > **Prospect**: *"I mean, years at this point."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"So why change now and not just continue doing the same thing you've done for the past 200 dots?"*
  > **Prospect**: *"Because then I'm going to be stuck paying rising bills indefinitely and not feeling in control."*
  > **Salesperson**: *"So what do you feel you need to do to make sure next time one of those dots gets circled, you've done something to stop being controlled by another company?"*

* **Reframe 4: The Island Reframe**:
  > **Salesperson**: *"Picture yourself on an island surrounded by water. If your goal is to get on top of a mountain where you see the oceans, rivers, and trees clearly, there are three steps: 1. Believe there is a mountain out there. 2. Strap on the shoes. 3. Start fucking swimming. You can spend another 2 weeks, 2 months, 2 years on this island—the view is not going to change north, south, east, or west. What do you feel you have to do to get off this island to that mountain?"*

## SECTION 5: FOLLOW-UP, REFERRAL & UPSELL SYSTEMS

### 5.1 Pipeline & Follow-Up System
* **Daily Block**: Reserve 30 minutes every day exclusively for pipeline management.
* **Categorized Columns**: Group unclosed leads into 8-week, 6-week, 4-week, 2-week, and 1-week columns.
* **Pipeline Progression**: Focus daily activity on moving leads leftward across columns (8-week → 6-week → 4-week → 2-week → 1-week → closed).
* **Touch-point Execution**: Execute 2–3 brief value touch-points per week (sending case study videos or specific trainings). Keep calls brief (30–45 seconds) rather than engaging in long 1-hour conversations. Always schedule a definitive follow-up call before hanging up.

### 5.2 Referral Engine (Success Points)
Ask for referrals at exact **success points** in the customer journey (Day 1 of install, Day 10 of power turn-on, Day 35 of first $0 bill):
* **Verbatim Script**:
  > *"Who else do you feel like you know, [Name], that could be struggling with high electric bills kind of like you were?"*
  > *"My big goal for this month is to help 5 more homeowners in the neighborhood—who else do you feel like you can introduce me to?"*

### 5.3 Upselling System (Battery Backup / Smart Add-Ons)
1. **Identify the New Gap**:
   > *"Walk me through now, what do you feel like you want more help with to get to that next level of power security for your home?"*
2. **Consequence & Pitch**: Build a consequence on not protecting the home against utility blackouts, then introduce the Unique Value Proposition (UVP) of adding battery storage (offering more efficiency, greater security, or higher likelihood of full energy independence).
