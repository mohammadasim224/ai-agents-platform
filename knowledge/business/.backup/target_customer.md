# Target Customer


## Primary Customer
The primary customer is a U.S. single-family homeowner who is responsible for paying the household electricity bill and is open to evaluating residential solar.

## Sample Geographic Profile
Primary sample market: homeowners in Arizona, especially the Phoenix metropolitan area.

Geographic references must be updated when targeting another state, city, or utility. Agents must not assume that Arizona utility programs or economics apply elsewhere.

## Basic Characteristics
- Owns a single-family home
- Pays the household electricity bill
- Has enough electricity usage for solar to be worth evaluating
- Has a property that may be suitable for solar
- Is financially able and willing to consider a solar project if the numbers make sense
- Is interested in reducing or better controlling long-term electricity expenses

These are targeting assumptions, not guaranteed qualification criteria.

## Core Pain Points
### 1. Rising Electricity Costs
Homeowners may be concerned that their electricity expenses will increase over time.

### 2. Lack of Control
Some homeowners dislike being dependent on utility-rate changes they cannot control.

### 3. Uncertainty About Solar
Many homeowners are interested in solar but do not understand system sizing, incentives, financing, utility rules, or the actual economics.

### 4. Contractor Trust
Homeowners may worry about aggressive sales tactics, misleading promises, poor installation quality, or choosing the wrong contractor.

### 5. Upfront Complexity
Homeowners may find solar proposals difficult to compare because they contain equipment, financing, production, utility, and incentive information.

## Desired Outcomes
A prospect may want to:
- Understand whether solar is financially sensible for their home
- Reduce exposure to future electricity-rate increases
- Better understand their monthly and long-term energy costs
- Evaluate whether incentives apply to them
- Understand available financing options
- Choose a reputable solar provider
- Make a decision using clear numbers rather than hype

## Common Objections / Concerns
- "How much does it cost?"
- "Will this actually save me money?"
- "Is solar really worth it?"
- "What happens if I move?"
- "What happens if the utility changes its rates?"
- "What if my roof is shaded?"
- "I don't want to get stuck in a bad contract."
- "I've heard solar companies make exaggerated claims."
- "I need to talk to my spouse."
- "Send me some information first."
- "I don't know if I qualify."

## Buying Triggers
Potential triggers include:
- Increasing utility bills
- A recent large electricity bill
- Interest in home improvement or energy independence
- Learning about a local utility-rate change
- Hearing about solar from a friend or local source
- Curiosity about incentives
- Concern about long-term electricity costs

These are potential triggers, not facts that should be asserted about a particular prospect.

## What Resonates
- Clear explanations
- Local or utility-specific information when verified
- Simple comparisons and calculations
- Transparent qualification criteria
- Educational content
- Specific answers to common objections
- Practical next steps
- A low-pressure consultation

## What Does Not Resonate
Avoid:
- Fake testimonials
- Fake personal stories
- "Free solar" claims
- Guaranteed savings
- Universal dollar amounts presented as if they apply to everyone
- Fake scarcity
- Government-payment claims without verified support
- Overly aggressive pressure
- Technical jargon without explanation

## Qualification Signals
A prospect becomes more relevant when they:
1. Own the home.
2. Are responsible for the electricity bill.
3. Are willing to discuss their current utility costs.
4. Are open to evaluating solar.
5. Meet any actual geographic, property, financing, or program requirements documented by the business.

Agents must not invent qualification criteria that are not documented in the business knowledge.

## Persona Guidance for Marketing
For AI-generated or scripted marketing content, use transparent educational personas such as:
- Energy researcher / market analyst
- Consumer advocate / industry commentator
- Educational presenter / direct guide

Do not write as though a fictional character personally installed solar, received a specific savings result, or is a real customer unless the claim is supported by an actual, authorized customer source.
